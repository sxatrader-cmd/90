Posterior Composite (Classes I and II), and Patient Assessment and Treatment Planning

English slides with a detailed, very simple Iraqi Arabic explanation of Lecture 15 by Prof. Linz (PDF pages 133–138) on posterior composite, and Lectures 1–2 by Dr. Abdullah Al-Shammaa (pages 139–147) on patient assessment, diagnosis and treatment planning.

  • Class I and II composite versus amalgam: no extension for prevention, a remaining 2 mm marginal ridge, and usually a 1–2 mm depth
  • Increments of no more than 2 mm, a 4–5 mm bulk fill, and a sandwich when the gingival margin extends beyond the CEJ
  • Caries detection without a sticky explorer, pulp tests, and a biologic width of about 2–3 mm
  • Cracked-tooth syndrome at 33–50 years, transillumination and a biting test, cervical burnout, and treating the most acute tooth first
  • Total run time: 38 minutes.
